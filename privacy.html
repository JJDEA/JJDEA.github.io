Summary

Calendar Widget runs entirely on your own Windows computer. It reads and changes your Google Calendar only to show and manage your schedule on your desktop. Your data is never sent to the developer, never sold, and never shared with anyone.

Data the app accesses

When you sign in with Google, you grant these permissions:

  • Basic profile and email address (openid, email, profile), used only to label each connected account in the app.
  • Your list of calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly), used so you can choose which calendars to show.
  • Events on your calendars (calendar.events), used to show events and to create, edit or delete events when you ask it to.

How data is stored

  • Your Google sign-in tokens are stored only on your computer, encrypted with Windows Data Protection (DPAPI).
  • A copy of recent events is cached on your computer, so the widget can show your schedule while you are offline.
  • Your settings (theme, chosen calendars, window position) are stored on your computer.
  • The app has no server. It communicates only with Google's own services (googleapis.com and accounts.google.com).

Sharing

Calendar Widget does not share, transfer, sell or disclose Google user data to any third party. It does not use Google user data for advertising, and it does not use it to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.

Calendar Widget's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

  • In the app, go to Settings → Accounts → Remove. This deletes that account's tokens from your computer and revokes the app's access.
  • You can also revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Uninstalling the app and deleting the %APPDATA%\Calendar Widget folder removes all locally stored data.
